Authorized distributor (Mouser/DigiKey) for the Nucleo board — clones exist with different USB-to-serial chips and flash configs that will burn debugging hours later. Pine Store direct for the soldering iron — beats Amazon clones. Amazon is fine for commodities (jumper wires, breadboard, hookup wire) where the part is what it says it is.

Cortex-M4 with FPU. The FPU matters when you write the Kalman filter in Month 7. Built-in ST-Link debugger. From authorized distributor only — Amazon clones will cost you weekends.

MG90S Metal-Gear Servos (×2)
Buy two. You'll burn one out learning. Metal gears outlast the SG90 plastic-gear version by enough to matter. Generic brand on Amazon is fine — these are commodity.

5V 3A Wall Adapter, Barrel Jack
3A, not 2A. Servos draw current spikes. Critical: never power servos from the Nucleo's 5V pin — current spikes will brown out the MCU and corrupt your firmware. Separate supply, common ground.

§ 02

Tools (One-Time)

subtotal $0 / ~$210
Buy-once principleThe cheapest tool is the one you don't replace. Skip $15 soldering kits and $5 multimeters — they're frustrating, they lie, and you'll buy quality versions anyway. The list below is mid-range, lifetime-purchase territory.
Pinecil V2 Smart Soldering Iron
Best $26 in hobby electronics. Heats fast, holds temperature, USB-C PD powered. Will outlive this project. Pine64 ships from US warehouses; lead time ~1 week.
QTY 1 Pine Store →
~$26
USB-C PD Power Supply (65W) for Pinecil
Pinecil needs USB-C PD at 65W to hit full temperature quickly. Skip if you already own a laptop charger that supports PD 65W+ (most modern ones do).
QTY 1 (skip if owned) Amazon →
$0–15
60/40 Leaded Solder, 0.6–0.8mm Rosin Core
Easier to learn with than lead-free. Use ventilation. Kester or MG Chemicals are reliable brands. One small spool lasts most of the project.
QTY 1 spool Amazon →
~$15
Klein MM325 Multimeter (or AstroAI ~$22 budget option)
Klein MM325 (~$30) is the buy-once option. AstroAI (~$22) is acceptable. Avoid sub-$15 meters — they lie about voltage and you'll debug ghost problems for weeks.
QTY 1 Klein → AstroAI →
$22–30
Helping Hands with Magnifier
You have two hands. Soldering needs three. Even the cheap ones work. The magnifier is more useful than you'd expect.
QTY 1 Amazon →
~$15
Klein 11061 Self-Adjusting Wire Strippers
Cheap strippers mangle wires and waste time. Self-adjusting handles 10–24 AWG without measuring.
QTY 1 Amazon →
~$25
Hakko CHP-170 Flush Cutters
The standard. Lasts forever if you don't try to cut steel with them. For trimming component leads after soldering.

~$8
iFixit Mako Precision Driver Kit (64-bit)
Hex, Torx, Phillips, JIS. Opens absolutely everything you'll encounter — Nucleo screws, servo cases, your kids' broken toys. Lifetime tool.

Digital Calipers (150mm)
Critical for designing 3D-printed brackets in Month 5. Cheap digital calipers are accurate enough for hobby mechanical work. Battery-included.

Solid core for breadboards. Multi-color matters for keeping power, ground, and signal visually distinct. Color discipline saves debugging time.
